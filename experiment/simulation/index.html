Theory & Circuit Architecture

1. The 50 Hz Power Line Interference Problem

In biomedical instrumentation (ECG, EEG, EMG), patient signal voltages are microscopic (typically 0.5 mV to 2.0 mV for ECG, and 10 µV to 100 µV for EEG). The human body acts as an antenna capacitively coupled to the 230V/50Hz mains power grid wiring, inducing severe 50 Hz displacement currents into the recording leads and chassis ground loops.

2. Galvanic Isolation Barrier (Optocoupler / Isolation Amp)

Galvanic isolation physically breaks the direct galvanic pathway between the patient-connected front-end and the mains earth ground. Using optical isolation (optocouplers / linear optical barriers) or capacitive isolation:

Common Mode Rejection Ratio: CMRR (dB) = 20 log₁₀(A_d / A_cm) > 120 dB

This isolates the floating ground, completely eliminating ground loop circulating hum and ensuring critical patient electrical safety compliant with IEC 60601-1.

3. Active Twin-T & Digital IIR Biquad Notch Filter

The remaining differential 50 Hz interference is eliminated by a high-Q notch filter centered at \(f_0 = 50\text{ Hz}\). In discrete-time digital signal processing, a 2nd-order IIR Biquad Notch Filter has the transfer function:

H(z) = b₀ · (1 - 2cos(ω₀)z⁻¹ + z⁻²) / (1 - 2r·cos(ω₀)z⁻¹ + r²z⁻²)

Where \(\omega_0 = 2\pi f_0 / f_s\) is the normalized center frequency, and \(r = 1 - (\pi \Delta f / f_s)\) controls the pole radius and filter quality factor \(Q = f_0 / \Delta f\). A sharp notch (\(Q \approx 16\)) eliminates 50 Hz hum while leaving adjacent ECG diagnostic features (P, QRS, T waves) undamaged!
